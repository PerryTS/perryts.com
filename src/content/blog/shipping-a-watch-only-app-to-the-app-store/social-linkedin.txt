============================================================

How do you ship a standalone Apple Watch app to the App Store? Turns out: not with any of Apple's own tools.

I just shipped dB Meter — a sound-level meter for Apple Watch, compiled from a single TypeScript file with Perry and running natively with no iPhone companion.

Getting it to build and run on the watch was the easy part. Getting it into the App Store took a full day of dead ends, because a "standalone watchOS app" is something Apple's delivery tooling quietly doesn't support:

• altool has no watchOS product type
• iTMSTransporter rejects the watchOS platform alias
• xcodebuild's export step offers no App Store method for a watch archive
• Xcode 26's Organizer shows only Ad Hoc, Enterprise, and Development

The usual escape hatch — embedding the watch app inside a real iOS app — would have forced me to strip arm64_32, the 32-bit slice every Apple Watch Series 4 through 8 and the SE needs. It also makes the App Store demand iPhone screenshots for an app that does nothing on iPhone.

The fix almost nobody documents: wrap the watch app in a tiny iOS "container stub" flagged ITSWatchOnlyContainer, keep the watch app WKWatchOnly with its arm64_32 slice intact, and upload with iTMSTransporter. App Store Connect then treats the whole bundle as watch-only — no iPhone screenshots, full support for older watches.

I wrote up the whole thing — the exact Info.plist keys, the three validation landmines, and the error each one throws:
https://perryts.com/blog/shipping-a-watch-only-app-to-the-app-store

If you're building for Apple Watch and Xcode is telling you it can't be done — it can.

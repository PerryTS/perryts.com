1/
I shipped dB Meter — a sound-level meter for Apple Watch. Standalone, no iPhone app, compiled from one TypeScript file with Perry.

Building it was easy. Getting it into the App Store took a full day, because Apple's tools quietly refuse a watch-only app.

2/
Every upload path fails on a standalone watch .ipa:
• altool → "Cannot determine the platform"
• iTMSTransporter → "Unknown platform alias: watchOS"
• xcodebuild export → no app-store method
• Xcode 26 Organizer → Ad Hoc / Enterprise / Development only

3/
The obvious fix — wrap it in an iOS companion app — has a trap: an iOS binary can't contain arm64_32, so you'd drop every Series 4–8 and SE watch. And the store then demands iPhone screenshots for an app that does nothing on iPhone.

4/
The real trick: an iOS *container stub* flagged ITSWatchOnlyContainer + LSApplicationLaunchProhibited, with the watch app (WKWatchOnly, arm64_32 + arm64) nested in Watch/. Upload with iTMSTransporter → the store treats it as watch-only. No iPhone screenshots, 32-bit kept.

5/
Three landmines: a swiftc stub gets rejected as a "beta SDK" (used Perry's own iOS output instead), the watch app needs the full CFBundleIcons dict — not just the key — and the stub needs UIRequiredDeviceCapabilities=[arm64].
